Three writing systems, a 16px floor that cannot move, and a product number people read aloud across a shop counter. The type here has more constraints than most projects and it is currently being treated as if it had none.

3 · Three-script optical harmony

Devanagari and Tamil have larger notional em boxes but sit optically smaller than Latin at the same pixel size, and both need more leading because of the marks above and below the baseline. Setting all three at 22px / 1.6 makes the Hindi and Tamil pages look quieter than the English one — which is exactly backwards for the audience.

Do not go below the floor. These scale up from 16px, never down — the 16px minimum is a hard constraint and tuning must only ever add.

The most under-appreciated item in the lab.

4 · The story versal

Initial capital on the maker's story

::first-letter
Now

My grandmother taught me this border when I was eleven. It takes four days to set the warp and another six to finish. The pattern is called mayil kan — peacock's eye.

With versal

My grandmother taught me this border when I was eleven. It takes four days to set the warp and another six to finish. The pattern is called mayil kan — peacock's eye.

Script caveat: ::first-letter on Devanagari or Tamil can split a conjunct or orphan a vowel mark and produce nonsense. Gate it to html[lang="en"] until it has been checked with a native reader for each script.

English only for now. Genuinely ask before enabling elsewhere.

Two I considered and rejected. A display serif for headings would fight the three-script requirement — you would need a matched Devanagari and Tamil serif, and the pairing will be wrong in at least one script.
