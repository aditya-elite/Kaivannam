Where the UI can go next

The motion and geometry spec is already built — shear, rough edges, the diagonal spine, the seal, the weave, stamp-in reveals. This lab is about the layer after that: what would make Kaivannam look like it was made by a person rather than assembled from a component library.

How to read this. Every proposal carries a cost tag and a verdict, and several verdicts are don't. A list where everything is a good idea is a list nobody thought about. The demos are live — hover them, press them, and turn on your OS reduced-motion setting to see what survives.

The shortlist, ranked

Ordered by visible change per hour spent, not by how interesting they are to build.

#IdeaCostWhy it matters here
1 Tabular numerals + stencilled product IDs 1 line Product IDs are read aloud in shops and typed off printed tags. Proportional digits make KS-4F7Q jitter.
2 Khadi paper grain ~0.4KB The base is currently a flat dot grid. Real cloth has fibre. Biggest look change for the least code.
3 Hairline form fields CSS only §2.4 says rules over boxes, but the registration form is still all boxes. The spec is half-applied.
4 Dye-lot colour variance seeded util Extends the rough.ts idea from shape to colour. A grid of cards stops reading as stamped.
5 Per-script optical sizing 3 rules Devanagari and Tamil sit optically smaller than Latin at the same px. Hindi pages currently look timid.
6 Wax-seal tier badge 1 component Trust tier is the most consequential fact on an artisan card and it looks like a generic pill.
7 Scroll-linked kolam progressive Ties the divider to the reader's own pace. Degrades to the current IO version where unsupported.
8 Printable tag furniture print CSS Cut marks, fold line, a signature rule. The tag is a physical artefact and currently prints like a web page.
9 Pointer-tilt on cards JS + risk Reads as generic SaaS, does nothing on touch — which is the entire target device. Listed so it can be ruled out.
10 Counting stat numbers a11y risk Animating a number that a screen reader is mid-way through announcing. Tempting, wrong.

What I'd actually do first

If there is one afternoon available, spend it on 1, 2 and 3. They touch every screen, cost almost nothing to run on a low-end phone, and none of them can break a state machine. Items 4–8 are a second pass. Items 9 and 10 are in the list so that when someone suggests them later there is a written answer.

Where I'm not confident. The dye-lot variance (4) is the one I'd want to see on a real device before committing — a hue jitter that reads as "handmade" on a calibrated laptop can read as "broken CSS" on a cheap LCD in daylight. Build it behind a flag and look at it outdoors before deciding. Same caution for letterpress on the cream: it depends on screen gamma more than I'd like.
